    Hamiltonian Mechanics very briefly

    Total energy of a system = kinetic energy + potential energy


    The Hamiltonian function, H(q,p)H(q, p), represents the total energy of the system in terms of phase space pairs.
    With Hamiltonian Mechanics, we describe a system in terms of phase space pairs.
    q: position
    p: momentum
    Each system has its own function to describe its motion.

    Symmetry

    Conservation of energy leads to symmetry in the systems of motion
    Motion of the Ideal Mass-Spring in one dimension shows reflection symmetry
    Velocity of the mass has symmetry on either side of x = 0

    Symmetry on a Phase Space Plot

    Time Derivative Vector Field for the ideal mass-spring
    pp = momentum (or velocity)
    qq = position
    Larger ∣p∣\lvert p \rvert means moving faster
    Larger ∣q∣\lvert q \rvert means further from resting position
    Vector field shows clockwise rotation because pp > 0 means positive velocity (qq getting larger). Top half.
    When p<0p < 0 is negative velocity (qq getting smaller). Bottom half
    compressed ↔\leftrightarrow stretched
